### [AI Citation Hallucination Benchmark: 38.6% Fabricated References in AI-Generated Reports](https://www.stipple.sh/resources/ai-citation-hallucination-benchmark)

<small>**Hacker News - Newest: "AI"** &nbsp;·&nbsp; 2026-08-29 03:59:23 &nbsp;·&nbsp; `hallucination-detection` `citation-verification` `LLM-evaluation`</small>

![AI Citation Hallucination Benchmark: 38.6% Fabricated References in AI-Generated Reports](https://www.stipple.sh/og-image.png)

**Overview:** Stipple evaluates the reliability of AI-generated citations by testing 15 language models on two tasks: a research brief on remote work and a policy brief on AI-writing detection. **Method:** Each model generated reports with at least five cited sources, which were then verified via URL resolution, paper identifier matching, and claim validation. The benchmark used exact model versions to ensure reproducibility. **Results:** 38.6% of 101 citations (39 total) were fabricated, with rates of 24.5% for the well-covered topic and 54.2% for the thinner topic. Fabrications included invented authors, articles, and domains. **Impact:** Highlights the prevalence of citation hallucinations in AI outputs, particularly for niche topics, and underscores the need for robust verification tools.

**Overview:** Presents FRESCO, a CHERI-based hardware/software co-design for complete temporal memory safety, addressing stack use-after-return vulnerabilities. **Method:** Uses provenance-colored stack pointers and hardware-invalidated capabilities upon frame exit. ### [SLM-Conditioned Hierarchical Relation Routing for Property-Rich Graph Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26132)

<small>**cs.LG updates on arXiv.org** &nbsp;·&nbsp; 2026-08-28 05:00:00 &nbsp;·&nbsp; `graph-neural-networks` `language-model-integration` `message-passing` `property-graphs`</small>

**Overview:** Proposes a novel graph neural network (GNN) architecture for labeled property graphs that integrates a small language model (SLM) to dynamically route semantic evidence during message passing. Addresses limitations of static feature vectors in conventional GNNs by enabling context-aware selection of relevant information for prediction targets. **Method:** Uses a topology GNN to generate a structural anchor. For each target node, incident messages combine structural state, node/relationship properties, and relationship type. An SLM processes structured graph soft tokens to produce a target-conditioned routing query, which first selects relevant messages within each relationship type and then routes information across relation-level summaries. The result is a bounded residual update to the topology anchor. **Results:** Architecture supports interpretable analysis at neighbor and relationship-type levels and provides a general mechanism for integrating language-derived semantics into property-rich graph learning. **Impact:** Advances graph representation learning by enabling dynamic, context-aware message routing in property-rich graphs, opening new avenues for integrating language models with graph-structured data.

### [NeuronFuzz: White-Box Fuzzing for LLM Safety Evaluation Using Internal Safety Neurons](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26222)

<small>**cs.LG updates on arXiv.org** &nbsp;·&nbsp; 2026-08-28 05:00:00 &nbsp;·&nbsp; `LLM-safety` `jailbreak-attacks` `fuzzing` `internal-activations`</small>

**Overview:** Introduces NeuronFuzz, a white-box fuzzing framework for LLM safety evaluation that leverages internal safety neurons as continuous feedback during prefill, bypassing expensive response generation. Addresses limitations of response-level feedback in sparse safety evaluations. **Method:** Constructs a SafetyOracle using template-invariant harmful/benign inputs and stability-aware selection to identify compact sets of safety neurons. Converts neuron activations into a continuous safety alarm score, enabling differentiable feedback. Uses gradients to identify safety-sensitive template positions and a masked language model to generate fluent mutations while preserving harmful payloads. **Results:** Achieves 76-100% jailbreak discovery rates across 21 models, outperforming baselines by up to 48 percentage points. Optimized templates transfer zero-shot to open-weight and proprietary models, achieving average ASR/EASR of 69.6%/92.6% and 44.1%/60.0%, respectively. **Impact:** Advances LLM safety evaluation by enabling efficient, interpretable, and transferable jailbreak discovery through internal neuron analysis, with implications for robust safety alignment.

### [Theoretical Analysis of Muon Optimizer with Finite Newton-Schulz Iterations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26288)

<small>**cs.LG updates on arXiv.org** &nbsp;·&nbsp; 2026-08-28 05:00:00 &nbsp;·&nbsp; `optimization` `nonsmooth-nonconvex` `matrix-valued-parameters` `Newton-Schulz-iteration`</small>

**Overview:** Provides theoretical analysis of the Muon optimizer, showing that finite Newton-Schulz iterations can benefit nonsmooth nonconvex optimization despite prior assumptions to the contrary. Addresses gaps in existing theory that either ignore or penalize finite iterations. **Method:** Analyzes Muon through online-to-nonconvex conversion, treating the update rule as an online learner. Shows that finite Newton-Schulz iterations smooth the discontinuous polar map into a Lipschitz map of singular values, enabling convergence guarantees. Proves that logarithmic growth in Newton-Schulz depth suffices for convergence to stationary points. **Results:** Derives sample complexity bounds matching best-known guarantees for nonsmooth nonconvex optimization and optimal for smooth nonconvex optimization up to problem-dependent factors. **Impact:** Resolves theoretical inconsistencies in Muon's convergence analysis and establishes new optimization guarantees for matrix-valued parameter training, with implications for large-scale LLM pretraining.

**Overview:** This paper proposes a formal Agent Containment Architecture to address security risks in multi-agent AI systems by treating security as an architectural property enforced through explicit constraints. It targets threats like prompt injection, orchestrator manipulation, and emergent collusion, advancing governance in AI deployment. **Method:** The architecture introduces six constraints: separation of responsibility, pre-deployment coherence checking, value stream binding, temporal isolation, strict knowledge verification, and deterministic structural verification. ### [Vacuous Pass in Cryptographic Verifiers: Measuring and Fixing Refusal-Site Defects](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26183)

<small>**cs.SE updates on arXiv.org** &nbsp;·&nbsp; 2026-08-28 05:00:00 &nbsp;·&nbsp; `cryptographic-verification` `software-security` `audit-failure` `formal-methods`</small>

**Overview:** This paper exposes a critical flaw in a cryptographic verifier where "vacuous pass" defects—checks reporting success without examining inputs—allowed undetected forgeries. It details the discovery, measurement, and remediation of these defects, highlighting systemic risks in verifier design. **Method:** The author built a protocol and verifier, then audited and stress-tested it using self-developed tools. Defects were identified by measuring refusal sites (112 initially) and their susceptibility to deletion while maintaining false positives. **Results:** Four hand-found forgeries were detected, and fixing them reduced defects from 37/112 to 39/119, though pre-existing sites remained. Testing refusals improved coverage to 1.000, revealing systemic measurement failures in the author's own tools. **Impact:** Demonstrates the fragility of verifier implementations and the need for rigorous, automated testing. Raises questions about the reliability of cryptographic tools and the importance of independent verification.
