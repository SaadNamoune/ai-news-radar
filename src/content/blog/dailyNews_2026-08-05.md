### [Probing and Defending Against Indirect Prompt Injection in Agentic LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02657)

<small>**cs.CR updates on arXiv.org** &nbsp;·&nbsp; 2026-08-05 05:00:00 &nbsp;·&nbsp; `prompt-injection` `agentic-llms` `security-defense` `linear-probing`</small>

**Overview:** This paper investigates indirect prompt injection (IPI) attacks on agentic LLMs, where malicious side-tasks are hidden in external tool results. The study examines the internal representations of LLMs during IPI exposure and proposes defenses to mitigate such attacks. **Method:** The authors employ linear probes trained on pre-generation hidden states to detect IPI exposure across six models, including a 753B-parameter model. They introduce AGRI, a probe-gated reasoning-based defense that prepends anti-injection reasoning to reduce attack success rates. The framework also includes an analysis to identify natural-language explanations correlated with probe-captured signals. **Results:** Linear probes achieve over 90% AUROC on unseen attacks and exhibit robustness under adaptive and cross-lingual settings. AGRI reduces attack success rates from 34.6% to 0% on Qwen3.5-27B while maintaining clean-task utility. **Impact:** The work advances the understanding of IPI attacks and provides a robust defense mechanism. It highlights the importance of internal signal analysis in LLM security and opens avenues for further research in dynamic, real-world deployment scenarios.

### [Zero-Knowledge Attestation for Fairness and Robustness in Regulated ML Deployments](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02664)

<small>**cs.CR updates on arXiv.org** &nbsp;·&nbsp; 2026-08-05 05:00:00 &nbsp;·&nbsp; `zero-knowledge-proofs` `fairness-attestation` `ml-regulation` `zkSNARK`</small>

**Overview:** This paper addresses the attestation problem for ML models deployed in regulated decision-making (e.g., credit underwriting) under U.S. banking guidelines (SR 11-7, OCC 2011-12). It proposes a chunked zero-knowledge circuit design to attest fairness and robustness metrics without exposing model weights or customer data. **Method:** The system attests demographic-parity gap and expected calibration error (ECE) on committed batches of HMDA mortgage data using zkSNARK proofs. A chunked design (32,768 rows, 32 proofs) ensures scalability, with per-chunk proving times under 4 seconds for fairness and 14.7 seconds for ECE. The authors also identify and resolve a data-quality failure (sentinel-code outlier) affecting circuit proving. **Results:** The attested fairness gap deviates by only 0.0029 from the true value, and ECE by 0.00037, demonstrating numerical precision. The chunked design outperforms flat summation (overflow) and tree-reduction (intractable) circuits. **Impact:** The work provides a scalable, privacy-preserving framework for ML attestation in regulated industries. It maps regulatory control language to zero-knowledge statements and proposes a threat model, offering a foundation for future auditing standards in ML deployments.

### [CHIARA: Hierarchical Allreduce for Small- and Medium-Message Regimes in Distributed Computing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02884)

<small>**cs.DC updates on arXiv.org** &nbsp;·&nbsp; 2026-08-05 05:00:00 &nbsp;·&nbsp; `mpi-collectives` `hierarchical-allreduce` `distributed-systems` `high-performance-computing`</small>

**Overview:** Addresses inefficiencies in MPI_Allreduce for small/medium messages by introducing *CHIARA*, a configurable hierarchical Allreduce that encodes hardware topology into a *batch-lane* logical structure. Targets latency, synchronization depth, and hardware hierarchy challenges in large-scale HPC and ML. **Method:** Implements a *staged schedule* where only a bounded portion of the reduction vector is active at a time. Uses *rotating-root lane* to distribute inter-batch communication without centralized leaders. Introduces *semi-composed Rabenseifner-style Allreduce* to preserve lane-aligned intermediate layouts, eliminating redundant intra-domain reorganization. **Results:** Achieves speedups of up to 1.94x (Polaris), 13.43x (Aurora), and 13.48x (Fugaku) over vendor MPI_Allreduce, with up to 2.2x end-to-end speedup in parallel k-means. **Impact:** Advances collective communication in distributed systems, reducing synchronization overhead and improving scalability. Open questions include generalization to other collectives and dynamic workloads.

**Overview:** This work develops a multimodal auto-regressive transformer surrogate to model variable well perforation and injection strategies for geological carbon storage (GCS) under geological uncertainty. The goal is to improve operational efficiency while accounting for complex subsurface dynamics. **Method:** The surrogate processes three input modalities—3D geomodel, scalar relative permeability parameters, and control variables—via separate encoders fused via self-attention in a transformer encoder. ### [GeoID-PINN: Physics-Informed Neural Networks for Regional SIRD Dynamics with Spatial Dependence](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02633)

<small>**cs.LG updates on arXiv.org** &nbsp;·&nbsp; 2026-08-05 05:00:00 &nbsp;·&nbsp; `physics-informed-neural-networks` `epidemiological-modeling` `spatial-dependence`</small>

**Overview:** Introduces GeoID-PINN, a physics-informed neural network (PINN) for SIRD dynamics that models spatial dependence using a row-stochastic source-composition matrix. Addresses challenges in disentangling local transmission, reporting, seeding, and external infection pressure in regional surveillance data. **Method:** The source-composition matrix assigns nonnegative source weights (summing to 1) to represent spatial dependence, regularized toward priors (distance, adjacency, commuting, etc.). Trained on synthetic and COVID-19 data from Louisiana counties, with trajectory fit and transmission-scale estimation. **Results:** In synthetic data, compatible distance prior reduces source-composition error from 0.159 (no regularization) to 0.099; misspecified prior increases error to 0.577. On 64 Louisiana counties, Forecast-Trained Geo-PINN reduces MSE from 32,957 to 11,468 and MAE from 70.60 to 57.73 vs. autoregressive baseline. County adjacency prior reduces MSE by 6.85% and MAE by 3.1%. **Impact:** Highlights the importance of spatial regularization in PINNs for epidemiology, showing that accurate trajectories do not guarantee correct dependence structure recovery. Opens avenues for structured uncertainty quantification in disease modeling.

### [Controlled Evaluation of Self-Evolving Skill Systems Shows Sparse, Feedback-Dependent Gains](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02636)

<small>**cs.SE updates on arXiv.org** &nbsp;·&nbsp; 2026-08-05 05:00:00 &nbsp;·&nbsp; `reinforcement-learning` `agent-evolution` `skill-learning` `benchmarking`</small>

**Overview:** This work evaluates self-evolving skill systems, which update agent skills based on execution feedback without altering the underlying model. It seeks to understand when evolution helps, how feedback shapes revision, and whether test-time computation can match gains. **Method:** A controlled framework evaluates 42 feedback runs across 14 model-benchmark settings, varying feedback types (successes, failures, or both). Skills are validated and tested for robustness and transfer. The study also analyzes eight models on SearchQA and compares evolved skills to test-time scaling methods like Parallel Sampling and Sequential Refinement. **Results:** Evolution is sparse: only 55 of 388 candidates improve validation bests. Validation-based selection chooses an evolved skill in 11 of 14 settings, with all selections requiring failed trajectories. Test-time scaling recovers only a fraction of gains (e.g., 0.43 vs. 30.96 points behind on SpreadsheetBench). **Impact:** Challenges assumptions about persistent skill improvement, framing evolution as sparse, validation-filtered search with model- and benchmark-dependent returns.
