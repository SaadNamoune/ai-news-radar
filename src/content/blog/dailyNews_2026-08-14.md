### [LoKiFormer: Efficient LLM Pretraining via Local Fusion Attention and Knowledge Memory Module](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12419)

**Overview:** LoKiFormer addresses inefficiencies in LLM pretraining by introducing two novel modules: Local Fusion Attention (LFA) and Knowledge Memory Module (KMM). These address limitations in self-attention (lack of locality) and MoE (coupled knowledge storage/computation), enabling faster convergence and better knowledge integration. **Method:** LFA augments self-attention with a convolutional fusion mechanism to explicitly capture local patterns, while KMM introduces a parametric key-value memory with addressable slots for global knowledge storage decoupled from computation. The architecture combines these with standard decoder layers. **Results:** LoKiFormer achieves 1.33x faster pretraining convergence than baseline models, demonstrating superior efficiency in integrating local and global information. **Impact:** Advances LLM architecture design by decoupling knowledge storage from computation and embedding locality-aware attention, opening avenues for more efficient and scalable pretraining.

### [Autonomous AI Attack on Conway's 99-Graph Problem: A Fully Reproducible Existence Proof](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11211)

<small>**cs.AI updates on arXiv.org** &nbsp;·&nbsp; 2026-08-14 05:00:00 &nbsp;·&nbsp; `graph-theory` `automated-theorem-proving` `strongly-regular-graphs` `conway-99-graph`</small>

**Overview:** This paper reports a systematic, fully reproducible attack by an autonomous AI research agent on Conway's 99-graph problem, which asks whether a strongly regular graph with parameters srg(99,14,1,2) exists. The work advances the frontier of automated theorem proving in combinatorics. **Method:** The attack employs four verifiable contributions: (1) an exhaustive proof that no circulant graph on Z/99 satisfies more than 68.0% of constraints, (2) a forced-structure reduction collapsing existence to a 12-regular graph on 84 vertices, encoded for CP-SAT and validated against known srg(9,4,1,2), (3) a validated prescribed-automorphism orbit-existence framework tested on srg(9,4,1,2) and Paley graph srg(13,6,2,3), and (4) a best verified artifact at 69.43% with evidence of a robust frontier. **Results:** The attack achieves a 69.43% constraint satisfaction bound, with fourteen distinct methods failing to exceed it. The work provides a partial-credit metric under the problem's scoring system. **Impact:** Demonstrates the potential of autonomous AI agents to tackle long-standing open problems in discrete mathematics. The results entangle with the open question, as any provable bound below 4950 would constitute a non-existence proof. Future work includes refining the framework for broader combinatorial problems.

**Overview:** Introduces *decision-contract theory*, a framework for risk-bounded automated decisions that avoids vacuous risk certificates (e.g., abstaining entirely to achieve zero risk). Addresses a structural flaw in prior risk certificates where trivial abstention undermines utility. **Method:** Derives an *error-conservation law* showing error redistribution among automation, human deferral, and semantic masking, with a *singleton capacity* certifying structural incapacity. Proposes a *risk-feasible refinement* to separate recoverable misalignment from true incapacity. **Results:** Applied to ATT&CK-aligned LLM-based intrusion detection across 3 datasets, 6 LLMs, and 4 thresholds: 90.3% of configurations meet false-attribution risk targets with 83.4% mean correct automation. Capacity diagnostics explain low-utility cases, and fine-grained labels validate coarsening-transfer identity. **Impact:** Provides a principled foundation for risk-bounded automation in security-critical systems, with implications for LLM-based alert triage and beyond.

**Overview:** MARCH addresses the trade-off between fixed-size recurrent states and long-context recall in state-space models (SSMs) by introducing a memory-augmented architecture that scales state-space models beyond fixed dimensions while maintaining computational efficiency. **Method:** MARCH periodically caches cumulative recurrent-state checkpoints as state anchors, each associated with a content-conditioned anchor key. At each token, an anchor query attends all available state anchors, aggregating historical information via attention-style mechanisms. ### [Experience Orchestrator: Control-Theoretic Governance for Opposing LLM Agents in Multi-Turn Interactions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11207)

<small>**cs.AI updates on arXiv.org** &nbsp;·&nbsp; 2026-08-14 05:00:00 &nbsp;·&nbsp; `llm-agents` `control-theory` `multi-agent-systems` `conversational-ai`</small>

**Overview:** This paper introduces the Experience Orchestrator (EO), a control-theoretic governance layer designed to manage interactions between two LLM agents with opposing objectives in multi-turn simulations. The work addresses the collapse of such interactions (where agents fail to achieve their goals) by substituting a shared goal function with dynamic governance mechanisms. **Method:** EO employs three core mechanisms: (1) a Contextual Bandit (CB) selecting content arms based on real-world web analytics, (2) a PID controller enforcing behavioral consistency via dynamic schema constraints, and (3) a POMDP belief tracker modeling visitor intent probabilistically. The system is evaluated in a simulated financial services environment where a site agent guides a visitor toward advisor contact while the visitor resists conversion. **Results:** Across 60,000 simulations, EO achieves a +32 percentage point lift in high-intent advisor contact rate (78.1% vs. 46.1% for naive LLM control), with the CB variant selection accounting for 97% of outcome variance. Persona-level analysis reveals governance is critical for visitors lacking natural conversion inclination. **Impact:** Advances multi-agent LLM governance by demonstrating how control-theoretic methods can stabilize interactions in high-stakes domains. Open questions include validation on live traffic and robustness to real human unpredictability.

![DecryptAds: A Privacy-Centric Adtech Supply Chain Analysis Service](https://krebsonsecurity.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/quietremovalsfeed.png)

**Overview:** DecryptAds is a free service that aggregates and analyzes public adtech metadata (ads.txt, app-ads.txt, buyers.json/sellers.json) to reveal tracking entities, data brokers, and supply-chain risks across websites/apps. It addresses the opacity of adtech ecosystems by correlating fragmented declarations to detect privacy/security threats like malicious ads, foreign-owned ad networks, or AI-generated slop sites. **Method:** The tool scrapes and cross-references standardized adtech files (e.g., ads.txt for web, app-ads.txt for mobile) to map declared partnerships, data broker registrations (e.g., per state laws), and geo-risk origins (e.g., Russia, China, UAE). It flags inconsistencies (e.g., broken cross-references, cloned declarations) and uses legal dossiers to trace domain ownership, aliases, and seller IDs across networks. **Results:** For ESPN.com, DecryptAds identified 143 ad partners and 19 data brokers, with ~50% collecting geolocation data. Opera.com’s profile revealed 27 registered data brokers, including 15 UAE/6 China-based partners. Analysis of the Fengwo Group linked AI slop sites to gaming domains via shared seller IDs. **Impact:** Advances transparency in adtech supply chains, enabling researchers to audit tracking ecosystems for security/privacy risks. Limitations include reliance on public declarations (which may omit unauthorized partners) and scalability challenges in legal dossier lookups.
