### [Agent Control Plane (ACP): A Security Architecture for Agentic Systems](https://github.com/yacine-kellib/agent-control-plane)

<small>**Hacker News - Newest: "llm"** &nbsp;·&nbsp; 2026-08-17 21:53:45 &nbsp;·&nbsp; `agent-security` `authorization-framework` `policy-engine` `formal-verification`</small>

![Agent Control Plane (ACP): A Security Architecture for Agentic Systems](https://opengraph.githubassets.com/4299ceb39c3e89e06e48f28c489c6859f262ee47ef7ac66eebcc92c5cd716988/yacine-kellib/agent-control-plane)

**Overview:** ACP introduces a security architecture for agentic systems to address authorization flaws where models lack the ability to distinguish instructions from data. It mitigates risks like poisoned documents or hostile inputs by separating policy enforcement from model execution. **Method:** ACP uses a policy engine to recompute risk from signed policies (unseen by the model) and an executor to verify or refuse actions. Human approval is required for irreversible operations. The system is verified via Ed25519 signatures, Dafny formal proofs, and 74 attack suites. **Results:** The demo shows ACP preventing unauthorized actions (e.g., data exfiltration) while allowing legitimate ones, with refusal based on policy (e.g., unregistered task types refused at 8.4-3). Human approval decay is measured but not fixed. **Impact:** Advances security in agentic AI by enforcing authorization as a policy fact, not a model capability. Open questions include scalability and real-world deployment challenges.

**Overview:** This paper critiques the overreliance on narrow coding benchmarks (e.g., SWE-bench) for claiming broad coding capabilities, highlighting a "meaning gap" between measured scores and general ability. **Method:** The authors construct a Django-based benchmark suite and evaluate post-trained models, showing that optimization for SWE-bench fails to generalize. **Overview:** This paper introduces the Lorentzian Fourier Neural Operator (L-FNO), a stochastic neural operator designed to model sparse, self-exciting event dynamics in operational systems. It addresses limitations of standard neural operators, which are trained as regression models rather than conditional-intensity estimators, making them unsuitable for rare-event regimes. **Method:** L-FNO combines an FNO-style covariate path with Lorentzian spectral kernels to capture history-dependent excitation. It uses a likelihood-based training objective to estimate conditional intensity, enabling better handling of bursty and self-exciting events. **Results:** Evaluated on eight synthetic and three real-world datasets (disease outbreak prediction, semiconductor fault detection), L-FNO outperforms regression- and likelihood-based baselines in event likelihood, calibration diagnostics, and rare-event detection. **Impact:** Demonstrates that structured spectral memory and likelihood-based learning provide effective inductive biases for modeling stochastic event dynamics, advancing neural operators for sparse regimes.

### [Activation-Prune-Merge: Cross-Scale Model Fusion Without Semantic Alignment](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13596)

<small>**cs.LG updates on arXiv.org** &nbsp;·&nbsp; 2026-08-17 05:00:00 &nbsp;·&nbsp; `model-fusion` `heterogeneous-models` `activation-guided-pruning` `transfer-learning`</small>

**Overview:** This paper introduces Activation-Prune-Merge (APM), a method for fusing a small recipient language model with a larger donor model despite architectural mismatches, without requiring neuron-wise semantic alignment. **Method:** APM constructs task-conditioned activation maps on the donor, prunes it to match the recipient's architecture, and injects the donor slice using a micro interpolation coefficient. This approach treats the donor as a source of functional components rather than requiring precise structural alignment. **Results:** On 16 benchmarks (reasoning, math, code, instruction-following, classification), APM improves the 3B recipient's average accuracy from 55.5% to 60.6%. Significant gains are observed in tasks like RTE (64.3%→82.3%), QNLI (52.3%→65.7%), and BoolQ (70.8%→79.2%). **Impact:** Demonstrates that cross-scale model fusion can succeed without explicit semantic alignment, enabling efficient transfer of capabilities from larger to smaller models.

### [RubricForge: Automatically Inducing Faithful Agent-Judging Rubrics for Reward-Free Evaluation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13564)

<small>**cs.AI updates on arXiv.org** &nbsp;·&nbsp; 2026-08-17 05:00:00 &nbsp;·&nbsp; `evaluation-methods` `language-model-agents` `automatic-judging`</small>

**Overview:** This paper introduces RubricForge, a method to automatically induce a faithful rubric for evaluating language-model agents without relying on environment rewards. It addresses the problem of unreliable automatic judges that often credit fluent but unsuccessful trajectories. **Method:** RubricForge evolves a judge rubric via reflective evolution against ground-truth-labeled trajectories to maximize agreement with environment rewards. The optimized rubric is frozen and applied to held-out trajectories in a single model call without environment access. The process is human-readable, ensuring attributable verdicts. **Results:** On tau-bench (173 labeled trajectories) and WebShop (160), RubricForge reduces false-pass rates by ~33% (0.115 vs. 0.173) compared to G-Eval, with zero reversals. While aggregate agreement is not statistically significant (p=0.248), faithfulness to true outcomes improves. **Impact:** Demonstrates that reward-free evaluators can prioritize deployment-relevant metrics (false-pass rate) over raw agreement, advancing trustworthy agent evaluation.

**Overview:** This paper investigates the theoretical limitations of adaptive snapshots in distributed systems, specifically whether invisible reads can be incorporated while maintaining linearizability. Adaptive snapshots are critical for recording state in multi-component systems, and invisible reads could improve performance. **Method:** The authors analyze the single-writer, single-scanner setting under assumptions satisfied by existing adaptive snapshot implementations. They prove that adaptive snapshots with invisible reads cannot be linearizable, using formal reasoning about concurrent operations. **Results:** The impossibility result holds even under restricted conditions, demonstrating a fundamental trade-off between performance optimizations (invisible reads) and correctness guarantees (linearizability). **Impact:** This work advances the theoretical understanding of snapshot algorithms in distributed systems, highlighting constraints in designing efficient and correct concurrent data structures. Open questions remain about alternative correctness criteria or relaxed consistency models.

**Overview:** This work addresses the challenge of translating C code to idiomatic, safe Rust using a fine-tuned LLM (Qwen3-27B) to eliminate memory-safety vulnerabilities while preserving functionality. **Method:** A three-stage curriculum fine-tunes the model: (1) continued pretraining on Rust corpora for idiomatic syntax, (2) supervised fine-tuning on Verus_Training_Data for debugging/repair, and (3) task-specific SFT on paired C/Rust solutions from LeetCode.
