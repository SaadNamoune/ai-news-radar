### [Deterministic Frontier-Scale Language Model Inference with Signed Receipts](https://zenodo.org/records/19882078)

<small>**Hacker News - Newest: "llm"** &nbsp;·&nbsp; 2026-08-25 21:24:36 &nbsp;·&nbsp; `deterministic-inference` `signed-receipts` `LLM-systems`</small>

**Overview:** This paper introduces a protocol for producing byte-identical outputs from frontier-scale language model inference (up to 72B dense or 47B MoE parameters) with offline-verifiable signed receipts. The work addresses reproducibility and verifiability challenges in AI inference, enabling issuer-independent fabrication soundness without hardware dependencies or zero-knowledge proofs. **Method:** The protocol consists of three components: (1) A deterministic inference substrate supporting cross-vendor execution (NVIDIA H100, AMD Instinct MI300X) with byte-for-byte identical outputs across configurations, including tensor-parallel setups where differences align with underlying collective communication topologies (NCCL vs RCCL). (2) A canonical CBOR receipt schema with Ed25519 signatures, implemented in Go, Python, and Rust, with verified cross-language byte-identity. (3) A probabilistic spot-check verifier with a proven soundness bound (1−(1−f)^k) validated across 700k Monte Carlo trials. **Results:** Verification costs ~80μs per receipt on a single core; 11,000 sequential warm inferences showed zero byte-identity failures. Cross-vendor receipt verification achieved byte-for-byte consistency even when processed on heterogeneous hardware. **Impact:** Advances AI systems research by enabling deterministic, verifiable inference at scale, with implications for auditing, reproducibility, and trust in AI deployments. Open questions include extending to larger model sizes and optimizing verification overheads.

### [Specialist Emission vs. In-Context Learning: A Four-Way Comparison Across Tasks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21386)

<small>**cs.LG updates on arXiv.org** &nbsp;·&nbsp; 2026-08-25 05:00:00 &nbsp;·&nbsp; `specialist-models` `in-context-learning` `few-shot-learning` `hypernetworks`</small>

**Overview:** This paper systematically compares four mechanisms for task specialization—zero-shot, in-context attention, test-time gradient adaptation, and *emission* of specialist weights via a hypernetwork—across six diverse tasks (regression, generation, language modeling, RL, clinical/genomic classification). The goal is to map the operating regimes where emission outperforms alternatives in cost-efficiency and quality. **Method:** The study fixes the specialist model, context, and training budget where possible, and evaluates emission’s ability to generate task-specific weights. For generation, a 132-float per-instance program is emitted; for regression, performance is compared to MAML under zero test-time gradient steps. In high-dimensional sequence modeling, a one-pass adapter and LoRA-rank sweeps assess capacity limits. Mechanism ablations validate task-conditioning and explore weight-space composition of specialists. **Results:** Emission ties TabPFN’s state-of-the-art on clinical few-shot classification while avoiding per-query support set re-attention, and achieves noise-floor shape generation with minimal parameters. On sinusoid regression, it outperforms MAML by 2–3 orders of magnitude at zero test-time steps, with a ~30× margin persisting under equalized budgets. However, in-context attention remains superior for sequence modeling: a one-pass adapter recovers only 14.0±0.9% of the in-context gain at 5M pre-training steps, improving to 21% with higher LoRA rank but still falling short. **Impact:** The work establishes emission as a cost-effective alternative for certain tasks, while highlighting its limitations in high-dimensional settings. It proposes a falsifiable thesis for mechanism selection based on task resolution, advancing research on dynamic model specialization and hypernetworks.

### [KVBoost: Chunk-Level KV Cache Reuse for Efficient LLM Prefill via Dual-Hash Keying and Repair Strategies](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21362)

<small>**cs.AI updates on arXiv.org** &nbsp;·&nbsp; 2026-08-25 05:00:00 &nbsp;·&nbsp; `kv-cache-optimization` `llm-inference` `attention-mechanism`</small>

**Overview:** KVBoost addresses the high prefill latency in transformer-based LLMs by enabling chunk-level KV cache reuse regardless of content position, overcoming limitations of prefix-caching systems. **Method:** It introduces a dual-hash keying scheme (prefix hash + content hash) for exact/approximate cache matching, with repair strategies (SelectiveRecompute, CacheBlendRecompute) to mitigate attention boundary errors. Additional optimizations include asymmetric KV quantization (int8/int4), adaptive chunk splitting, and importance-weighted eviction. **Results:** On Qwen2.5-3B over 1,000 samples, KVBoost achieves 4.49x faster time-to-first-token (142.4 ms vs. 639.1 ms) and 16% better than prefix caching, with 99.2% accuracy vs. 99.1%. **Impact:** Provides a practical, memory-bounded inference acceleration layer compatible with RoPE-based models without architectural changes, advancing efficient LLM serving.

**Overview:** This paper provides an empirical basis for selecting OSINT feeds to detect cyberattack precursors for critical infrastructure (CI), addressing the lack of evidence-based guidance. It matters because defenders rely on OSINT feeds without knowing their effectiveness. **Method:** The study audits 10 OSINT source classes across 54 confirmed CI cyberattacks (2010–2024) and 12 null-control cases, evaluating attack coverage, null-case contamination, and signal lead time. Precision is stable across temporal and geographic partitions. **Impact:** Advances CI defense by providing a data-driven framework for OSINT feed selection. Open questions include extending analysis to other sectors and improving feed robustness to contamination.

### [Sys: Power-Constrained LLM Serving with Flex SLO Contracts and KKT Optimization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21719)

<small>**cs.DC updates on arXiv.org** &nbsp;·&nbsp; 2026-08-25 05:00:00 &nbsp;·&nbsp; `llm-serving` `power-capping` `KKT-optimization` `DVFS`</small>

**Overview:** Addresses the challenge of LLM serving under time-varying power caps imposed by grid operators, where traditional static energy optimization fails. **Method:** Introduces \sys{}, a system with (1) a Flex SLO contract converting user slack into optimization constraints, (2) prefill--think--answer disaggregation with per-stage frequency and KV-cache control, and (3) a KKT-based online solver re-optimizing within 7.7 ms of power cap changes. A fail-safe power-gates instances when DVFS bottoms out. **Results:** On SGLang with production traces, \sys{} sustains 78.3% online goodput at a 30% power cap reduction (vs. 47.6% for best baseline), maintains latency tails within 1.3× nominal (baselines 2.3–12×), and delivers 92% mean goodput during a replayed grid emergency (baselines <7%). **Impact:** Advances LLM serving under power constraints, enabling grid-aware deployment with minimal performance degradation.

### [Runtime Action Interference: Controlling RL Policy Deployment Without Altering Parameters](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21398)

<small>**cs.LG updates on arXiv.org** &nbsp;·&nbsp; 2026-08-25 05:00:00 &nbsp;·&nbsp; `reinforcement-learning` `runtime-control` `human-study` `policy-deployment`</small>

**Overview:** This work introduces *runtime action interference* (RAI), a control mechanism for RL policies that regulates action output *after* inference without modifying policy parameters. RAI addresses deployment-time behaviors like pacing, filtering, or suppressing actions based on user-defined constraints (e.g., toxicity, rate limits). The paper evaluates RAI’s impact on human perceptions of an opponent in *StarCraft II* through a controlled study. **Method:** RAI implements cooldown conditions and content detectors (e.g., for harassment) to gate action release. The system is deployed in a human participant study comparing two conditions: one where RAI’s capability is withheld (claim withholding) and one where it is disclosed. The study measures perceived fairness, trust, and toxicity on a 1–5 scale. **Results:** Under claim withholding, pooled means were 3.90 (fairness), 3.50 (trust), and 2.00 (toxicity), versus 2.62, 4.31, and 2.85 under disclosure. Disclosure consistently lowered perceived fairness and increased perceived toxicity across expertise groups, while trust increased for novices/experts but decreased for intermediates. **Impact:** The findings demonstrate that user perceptions of controlled AI systems are highly sensitive to capability disclosure, underscoring the need to separate *control mechanisms* from *capability claims* in human-computer evaluations. RAI’s open-source implementation provides a template for safe RL deployment in interactive environments.

### [CC-GMNet-TS: Class-Conditioned Gaussian Mixture Quantifier for Imbalanced Time Series](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21473)

<small>**cs.LG updates on arXiv.org** &nbsp;·&nbsp; 2026-08-25 05:00:00 &nbsp;·&nbsp; `quantification` `time-series` `gaussian-mixture-models` `transformers`</small>

**Overview:** This paper addresses *quantification*—estimating class prevalences in unlabeled bags of time-series data—for imbalanced settings like biosignal monitoring or activity recognition. It introduces *CC-GMNet-TS*, a class-conditioned Gaussian mixture quantifier that assigns each class a compact, bounded latent mixture to emphasize rare patterns. **Method:** CC-GMNet-TS uses a Transformer-based feature extractor paired with per-class latent mixtures. Bags are constructed via the Artificial Prevalence Protocol (APP) and prior-shift sampling (PShift) to cover diverse prevalence scenarios. The model is trained end-to-end with a quantification-oriented loss. **Results:** On three benchmarks (EMG Gestures, SmartFallMM, UCI-HAR), CC-GMNet-TS achieves lower quantification error than traditional aggregators and recent deep quantifiers. Ablations confirm the Transformer backbone and class-conditioned mixtures improve performance under PShift. **Impact:** The work advances quantification for imbalanced time-series data, offering a scalable alternative to instance-level labeling. It opens questions about generalization to real-world non-stationary distributions and broader applicability to multimodal biosignals.

### [Hypothesis-Driven PII Localization in Mobile Forensic Databases Using Agentic Search](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21469)

<small>**cs.CR updates on arXiv.org** &nbsp;·&nbsp; 2026-08-25 05:00:00 &nbsp;·&nbsp; `forensic-analysis` `PII-localization` `agentic-framework` `SQLite`</small>

**Overview:** This paper introduces a hypothesis-driven agentic framework for discovering personally identifiable information (PII) in mobile forensic databases (SQLite), addressing challenges like unknown table-column regions, heterogeneous schemas, and embedded values. It matters because manual PII extraction is labor-intensive and error-prone, while automated methods must balance thoroughness with efficiency. **Method:** The framework treats PII localization as bounded, adaptive search under uncertainty.
