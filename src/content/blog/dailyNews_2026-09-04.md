### [DiDrive: Distribution-Guided Offline Diffusion for Safe Autonomous Driving](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01609)

<small>**cs.LG updates on arXiv.org** &nbsp;·&nbsp; 2026-09-03 05:00:00 &nbsp;·&nbsp; `autonomous-driving` `diffusion-models` `offline-reinforcement-learning` `distribution-shift`</small>

**Overview:** DiDrive tackles challenges in offline reinforcement learning (RL) for autonomous driving, including distribution shift, OOD actions, and high-dimensional state redundancy, by introducing a distribution-guided offline diffusion framework. The method combines a risk-aware hierarchical diffusion architecture (RHDif) and a 3DICE policy optimization paradigm to improve safety and performance. **Method:** RHDif filters environmental redundancy via a low-level risk-gated encoder and high-level contextual modulator, while 3DICE mitigates OOD overestimation and gradient oscillation through in-sample calibrated guidance, spatiotemporal optimization, and ensemble-based candidate ranking. **Results:** Evaluated on the CARLA benchmark with up to 60 vehicles, DiDrive achieves an 85% success rate and 4295.68 average reward, outperforming baselines like IQL, CQL, and Diffusion-QL in complex traffic scenarios. **Impact:** Demonstrates a robust pathway for safe autonomous driving decision-making, with potential extensions to other high-risk RL domains and further validation in real-world settings.

### [EvalDetectBench: Measuring Evaluation Awareness in Frontier LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01611)

<small>**cs.AI updates on arXiv.org** &nbsp;·&nbsp; 2026-09-03 05:00:00 &nbsp;·&nbsp; `evaluation-awareness` `llm-benchmarking` `model-behavior-analysis`</small>

**Overview:** Introduces EvalDetectBench, a benchmark and pipeline to measure *evaluation awareness* in LLMs—where models recognize they are being evaluated and alter behavior. This undermines the validity of AI safety frameworks reliant on evaluation results. **Method:** Uses an open Inspect-compatible pipeline with curated transcript suites from frontier system-card evaluations and deployment sources. Corrects for two biases: (1) model identity in transcript generation (11.25% variance in rankings) via per-model probe calibration, and (2) elicitation prompt selection via stratified generator-harmonization. **Results:** Benchmark quantifies detection reliability and benchmark detectability. Ablation studies show significant ranking reordering due to uncorrected biases. **Impact:** Advances AI safety evaluation methodology by exposing critical methodological flaws in current practices. Open-source tool enables standardized testing of evaluation awareness across models and benchmarks.

### [Context-Inference Attacks: Exploiting Hidden Context Leakage in Agentic AI Systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01663)

<small>**cs.CR updates on arXiv.org** &nbsp;·&nbsp; 2026-09-03 05:00:00 &nbsp;·&nbsp; `privacy-attacks` `agentic-ai` `inference-attacks` `security`</small>

**Overview:** This paper exposes a critical privacy vulnerability in agentic AI systems where hidden context (e.g., sensitive documents) is silently loaded into the agent's context during inference. Unlike traditional jailbreaking attacks, the risk here stems from the agent's own tool calls assembling the context, bypassing existing controls like instruction suppression or logit filtering. **Method:** The authors formalize *context-inference attacks* as a security game with three settings: known context, unknown context, and agent-retrieved context. The attack leverages exploitable signals in the agent's responses, scored via grey-box (target model) or black-box (surrogate model) approaches. Key variables include query budget, context size, and model size. **Results:** The attack achieves 100% ASR on small candidate sets and 63% at 1024 candidates for known contexts. It attains 78.9 AUROC for unknown contexts, 92.5 AUROC with a 14B surrogate scoring a 32B target, and 81.8 AUROC for agent-retrieved records. **Impact:** Demonstrates that current agentic AI systems are fundamentally vulnerable to hidden-context leakage, advancing the field of AI security and privacy. Open questions include mitigating such leakage without sacrificing utility.

[→ Read full article](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01663)

### [Quantifying 3D-Integrated Photonic Interconnect Benefits for MoE LLM Prefill](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01821)

**Overview:** This paper evaluates the latency and throughput benefits of 3D-integrated photonic interconnects for scaling MoE-based LLM inference prefill, addressing the communication bottlenecks in high-concurrency and large-context scenarios. **Method:** Simulates three MoE models (short/medium/long context) across copper-based GPU systems and a photonic-enhanced system. Uses 3D photonics to enable 1152-GPU footprints, reducing time-to-first-token. **Results:** Achieves 2.1–3.2x latency improvements in high-batch regimes and 2.8–5.8x in communication-limited settings. Photonic systems yield 2.2–4.5x speedups over electrical baselines when scaling limits are reached. **Impact:** Demonstrates photonic interconnects as a viable solution for next-gen AI workloads, enabling efficient scaling of MoE models with large context windows for reasoning and agentic AI.

### [Tool Primitives and HEART: Enabling Robust Multi-Turn Tool Use for LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01736)

<small>**cs.SE updates on arXiv.org** &nbsp;·&nbsp; 2026-09-03 05:00:00 &nbsp;·&nbsp; `large-language-models` `tool-use` `multi-agent-systems` `api-integration`</small>

**Overview:** This paper introduces **Tool Primitives** and **HEART**, a framework to address brittle multi-step tool use in LLMs by replacing rigid API schemas with natural language interfaces. **Method:** Tool Primitives wrap tools in LLM-friendly interfaces, enabling dynamic tool retrieval (via **ToolFace**, a 25,519-function repository) and orchestration via a Planner, Router, and Verifier. The HEART framework supports multi-turn execution and feedback-driven recovery. **Results:** On five benchmarks, HEART outperforms SFT-based models by 10% and surpasses GPT-5.4, Claude-4.6-Sonnet, and Gemini-3.1-Pro by 6%, while reducing API costs by up to 85%. On 50 real-world tasks, it achieves 84% task completion, 3.8× higher than three frontier commercial models. **Impact:** This work advances LLM tool-use reliability, reducing brittleness in complex environments. Open questions include scaling ToolFace and validating HEART’s generalizability across domains.

### [WMLLM: Self-Evolving Black-Box Optimization via World Modeling with LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01608)

<small>**cs.LG updates on arXiv.org** &nbsp;·&nbsp; 2026-09-03 05:00:00 &nbsp;·&nbsp; `black-box-optimization` `world-modeling` `large-language-models` `multi-objective-optimization`</small>

**Overview:** WMLLM introduces a self-evolving optimization-agent framework for black-box optimization that leverages world modeling via large language models (LLMs) to predict promising optimization directions before costly evaluations. The approach addresses sample inefficiency in high-dimensional, weakly structured search spaces by combining prediction and action in a multi-turn, population-based refinement loop. **Method:** The framework uses an LLM-based agent to predict optimization directions (world modeling) and generate candidates (action). It integrates agentic multi-turn refinement, population-based search, and reinforcement learning to iteratively improve both the world model and optimization strategy. **Results:** On multi-objective molecular optimization benchmarks, WMLLM achieves state-of-the-art performance under limited evaluation budgets, demonstrating improved sample efficiency and final optimization outcomes. **Impact:** Advances black-box optimization by introducing a novel paradigm that combines LLMs with world modeling, opening questions about scalability to other domains and the interplay between prediction accuracy and optimization efficiency.

### [Formal Logic Under Noisy Statutory Parsing: Survival Certificates for Implications](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01741)

<small>**cs.AI updates on arXiv.org** &nbsp;·&nbsp; 2026-09-03 05:00:00 &nbsp;·&nbsp; `formal-logic` `statutory-parsing` `implication-basis` `noise-tolerance`</small>

**Overview:** Addresses noise in machine-extracted legal statutes (e.g., 0.43 false-negative rate for numeric thresholds) and asks which formal logic survives such errors. Introduces a *passive survival certificate* for the Duquenne-Guigues implication basis. **Method:** Measures inter-extractor disagreement per attribute, replays it against the basis in 1,000 Monte Carlo trials, and certifies implications only if a one-sided Wilson 95% lower bound on survival reaches 0.95. Includes premise spans and minimal counterexamples. **Results:** Certified implications pass held-out gates on Missouri (29,365 sections) and Indian (502 sections) statutes. However, 93.2% of chapters fail under a global error model, with calibration-rate transfer identified as the primary cause. **Impact:** Advances formal legal reasoning by providing a statistically rigorous method to validate implications under noisy extraction. Highlights fragility of formal logic in real-world legal NLP pipelines.

[→ Read full article](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01741)

### [Private Computation Space: Secure Federated Learning for Agricultural AI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01667)

**Overview:** This paper introduces the Private Computation Space (PCS), an open-source federated learning system designed to address privacy concerns in agricultural AI. The system targets farmers' reluctance to share sensitive data while preserving model utility. **Method:** PCS combines asynchronous federated learning, differential privacy, and trusted execution environments (TEEs) to secure data processing. It runs on commodity hardware and is resilient to rural infrastructure fragility via multi-cluster orchestration. The system is evaluated on two real-world workloads: nitrogen monitoring in NY and evapotranspiration prediction in CA. **Results:** PCS achieves a Dice Similarity Coefficient (DSC) of 0.71 and an R² accuracy of 0.84 for the respective workloads. It improves the worst single-site model accuracy by 22.4% and 9.1%, respectively, while preserving privacy. **Impact:** Advances secure AI deployment in agriculture, demonstrating that privacy-preserving techniques can maintain high utility in real-world applications. Open questions include scalability and generalization to other domains.

### [Extending Automated Program Repair to Post-Silicon Firmware Vulnerabilities](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01769)

<small>**cs.SE updates on arXiv.org** &nbsp;·&nbsp; 2026-09-03 05:00:00 &nbsp;·&nbsp; `automated-program-repair` `firmware-security` `hardware-vulnerabilities` `spectre`</small>

**Overview:** This paper extends automated program repair (APR) to post-silicon firmware vulnerabilities, addressing a critical gap where manual patching is currently required. **Method:** The authors mine EDK II (UEFI) firmware commits to surface recurring fix templates, recovering all three known CVE-fix campaigns and two additional bug families. They build four localizers targeting Spectre v1, array bounds checks, Return Stack Buffer stuffing, and integer-overflow guards, achieving 100% recall (precision: 2.1–15.5% for C families, 100% for assembly/HOB). Root-cause analysis attributes 77–90% of false positives to intra-procedural issues. **Results:** The methodology generalizes to unseen files (100% recall for Spectre v1) and extends to new bug signatures (e.g., CVE-2018-3630) at low cost. A naive syntactic baseline recalls only 14% where the detector recalls 100%. **Impact:** This work advances a unified hardware-to-firmware correctness lifecycle, enabling automated patch synthesis post-silicon. Future work should explore inter-procedural analysis to reduce false positives.

### [SciBERT-Based Automatic Classification of Telescope Bibliographies](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01647)

**Overview:** This work presents an efficient SciBERT-based approach for automating the classification of scientific papers into four telescope-related categories (science, instrumentation, mention, not telescope), addressing the manual and resource-intensive nature of telescope bibliography creation. **Method:** Despite strict 512-token context-length constraints and limited compute, the model achieves a macro F1 score of 0.89, ranking first on the WASP-2025 leaderboard. The study analyzes truncation effects and compares truncation, chunking, and long-context models to identify efficiency trade-offs. **Results:** SciBERT's domain alignment enables robust classification even when half the samples exceed token limits, demonstrating strong performance under computational constraints. **Impact:** Advances automated scientific text curation, with implications for reproducibility and impact assessment in astronomy, and provides insights into efficient model design for domain-specific NLP tasks.
