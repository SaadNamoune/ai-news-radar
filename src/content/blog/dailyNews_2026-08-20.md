### [Complexity Gaps in LCL Problems: Unbounded Degree Networks and Translation Theorem](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18684)

<small>**cs.DC updates on arXiv.org** &nbsp;·&nbsp; 2026-08-20 05:00:00 &nbsp;·&nbsp; `distributed-algorithms` `LOCAL-model` `LCL-problems` `complexity-gaps`</small>

**Overview:** Resolves long-standing open questions about complexity gaps in locally checkable labeling (LCL) problems by showing they arise from bounded-degree assumptions, not local checkability. **Method:** Introduces the *Increasing Degree* problem and a *Translation Theorem* to transfer complexity results across ranges, proving new upper/lower bounds for unbounded-degree networks. **Results:** Demonstrates infinitely many LCL problems with round complexities in [ω(1), o(log log* n)] and [ω(log* n), o(log n)] under unbounded degrees, using deterministic algorithms in port-numbering LOCAL and lower bounds for randomized/quantum LOCAL, even on trees. **Impact:** Foundational result in distributed computing, extending Schmid (2026) and Bousquet et al. (2025), and reshaping understanding of LCL problem complexity in general graphs.

### [Instrument for Detecting Misspecified Hybrid PDE-Parameter Estimators](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16925)

<small>**cs.LG updates on arXiv.org** &nbsp;·&nbsp; 2026-08-20 05:00:00 &nbsp;·&nbsp; `PDE-inverse-problems` `model-misspecification` `parameter-estimation` `statistical-testing`</small>

**Overview:** This work presents an instrument to detect and distinguish misspecified hybrid PDE-parameter estimators from unidentifiable parameter scenarios using a single fit, addressing a critical gap in inverse problem validation. **Method:** An information-matrix statistic with plug-in scaling is proposed, evaluated on a self-adjoint parabolic inverse problem. The statistic achieves a median of 0.19 under correct specification (rejection rate 0.033 vs. ceiling 0.10) and rises to 224 and 85 under misspecifications. A rank statistic collapses to zero under non-identifiability (Clopper-Pearson [0.024, 0.090]). **Results:** The instrument separates misspecification from unidentifiability across designs, with the misspecified estimator's in-domain RMSE (2.7×10⁻²) masking errors (e.g., 29.7% coefficient deviation at zero noise). Neural estimators converge to pseudo-true values, while physics-informed networks diverge. **Impact:** Provides a deployable test for hybrid estimator validation, advancing reliability in inverse problems where traditional accuracy checks fail.
